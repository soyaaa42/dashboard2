Planning Analysis

Social Sector Planning Recommendation

Use this as the interpretation block for your dashboard.

Population & Households

Tarlac Province recorded 1,475,227 household members. The population profile uses 383,157 households, while food, finance, and safety indicators use 383,162 responding households. This five-record denominator difference is retained and clearly identified.

Education (TVET/SHS)

66.3% of TVET graduates and 45.2% of Senior High School graduates provincewide are employed. Municipalities with lower SHS working rates (e.g. Sta. Ignacia at 37.4%) need stronger job-matching and livelihood bridging for young graduates.

Senior Citizens

Of 163,748 senior citizens, 84.5% hold a Senior Citizen ID, 24,337 are recorded without one, and 993 have no reported ID status. San Jose, Anao, and Capas have the lowest ID coverage and should be prioritized for mobile registration drives.

Youth and Welfare

25.6% of youth (69,395 of 270,916) are not in employment, education, or training (NEET). 25.9% of households worry about having enough food, while only 60.0% have any formal financial account — signaling a need for combined livelihood, food security, and financial-inclusion programming.

Recommended Priority Action

  • Prioritize senior citizen ID mobile registration in San Jose, Anao, and Capas, where ID coverage trails the provincial average.
  • Target youth NEET reduction programs in Bamban (31.5%) and La Paz (28.6%), the highest not-in-engagement rates.
  • Pair TESDA/TVET placement support with municipalities showing low graduate working rates, especially Sta. Ignacia.
  • Expand financial-inclusion and food-assistance programs jointly, since both indicators point to overlapping vulnerable households.
  • Continue barangay-level CBMS updates to track these indicators against future poverty-reduction targets.
